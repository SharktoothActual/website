# Nothing is sacred 
I realize I am some smaller-than-microscopic nobody shouting into the void that is the internet, but recently I came across something that bothered me.
I know, truly shocking that I would find something on the *internet*, of all places, that *bothered me*.
Nonsense and self-awareness aside, I'm sure We can all agree that everything sucks these days...
Granted, some things less than other things, but ultimately everything does at least *kinda* suck right now.
But as for me? Personally? I'd say that I think it's people that suck the most.
In fact, I'd argue that some people suck so much that it's almost looped around and become a point of pride for them.
You've used social media, you know the exact types of people I'm talking about.
That's right, I'm talking about the **exact group** of people we don't like.
It's definitely the political subcategory you're thinking of and not the opposite one.

### So now that we're on the same page, please allow me to share my observations today.

I came across a small Linux distro that looked pretty promising:
Based on Devuan, very lightweight, simple Xfce desktop that didn't look awful, basically my type of distro.
This distro kind of had everything going for it and I was getting ready to dump a copy of it into a VM to test it out.
But of course, if something is too good to be true... it isn't true.
I kept reading a bit more about the distro and noticed something a bit unusual on the description page.
Specifically, it said something about "no politics."
Being ever the optimist, I assumed this just referred to the team making it just being very focused.
Politics in Linux usually amounts to heated debates that result in forks, which often aren't good for distros.
There's a case for the debate being a good thing, but I prefer a distro that is focused and will last for years.
So, on that note, "no politics" seems like something I could certainly get behind.
But of course, nothing can ever be that simple...

Sure enough, the moment I began to dig a little bit deeper I promptly discovered a related project that kept notes of politics in software.
But not *Linux* politics, **American** politics. The stuff that affects me, as an American. Straight-up, raw *poop from a butt.*
Truthfully, I do appreciate someone taking the time to do such a thing as politics matters.
However, that project was heavily biased in such a way that *clearly* tried to villainize one perspective.
This is, objectively, dishonest. It's one thing to track political taint in open source projects, it's another thing to use it to soapbox.
I was lured in by the promise of apolitical software only to realize that it's actually a product of political nonsense.
The worst part is this isn't even a one-off thing, this is just something I happened to spot today.
It certainly seems to me that **projects advertising "no politics" is usually an indicator that there will be politics**.
And where there's politics, there will be tears.

### Stop pissing in my corn flakes and let me enjoy stuff

Forgive me if I'm mistaken, but "no politics" should just mean exactly that: No politics.
If I can read between the lines and find politics, then that's not "no politics," that's just politics with extra steps.
You're not sneaky or clever, you're a conniving little rat and you should go take a long walk off a short pier.
I shouldn't be able to figure out your political leanings based on anything written on your project page. Period.
My point being that as long as you have software I need, I will support and use the software you make.
We'll have a great thing going, just leave it at that and we can be internet buddies happily making the world suck less.
I don't need you telling me what to think, and I don't need you telling me what I currently think is wrong.
Hell, you don't even need to *know* what I think because **it just doesn't freakin' matter**.
Your experience in life is as valid as mine, don't give me a reason to think otherwise.

## Life could be a dream, but ya'll wanna play games

Let's take two prominent pieces of software that could rule the world if they collaborated:
In one corner is the GNOME Foundation and their desktop environment, named GNOME.
They are known for being overtly hostile towards a group of people who do not share their political beliefs.
As a result GNOME now has a cult devoted to hating and shaming it, accusing them of being elitist.
Nobody talks about how GNOME basically set the standard for the modern Linux desktop we know and love.
Nobody talks about how GNOME consistently delivers a consistent and stable desktop environment between major upgrades.
You could write a small novel on all the fantastic things GNOME has done and it's overshadowed by one political incident.

In the opposite corner is Hyprland, another desktop environment that serves as an alternative to GNOME.
They are known for being overtly "anti-political" and frequently ostracize and remove members who do not share their political beliefs.
As a result, Hyperland has established itself as a window manager/DE that people should steer clear of due to said politics.
Nobody talks about how Hyprland was the first example of a truly fully-featured tiling window manager for Wayland.
Nobody talks about how the lead developer is legitimately a fantastic developer any way you look at it.
You could sing praises of Hyprland all day and still not cover the basics, but nobody does because they're too open political beliefs.

Despite these to projects having completely opposite political philosophies, they both contain aspects that fit together *wonderfully*.
Every program designed for GNOME brings a level of consistency and fludity to Hyprland, and it all fits together without any fuss.
All of the keyboard shortcuts from Hyprland give the GNOME desktop a level of polish and usability that **just feels right**.
If the denizens of both of these communities ever interacted with each other they'd create the ultimate desktop, I swear by that.
**But they won't talk to each other, because they HATE each other.**
All thanks to nonsense that has *nothing* to do with software.

If you find that hard to believe then I recommend you dump Fedora in a VM and configure all the Hyprland keybinds on it.
Try using it, you'll see what I mean within minutes.
More of a Hyprland kind of person? Great, dump CachyOS + Hyprland in a VM, install Flatpak, then install the GNOME Circle apps.
Use those apps on there and note how everything just fits together with no fuss.
It's almost like I ignored the politics and saw a light nobody else is seeing.
Now that you've seen how good a Linux desktop can be with so little effort, share it with the masses.
**And don't say a friggin' word about politics when you do.**
